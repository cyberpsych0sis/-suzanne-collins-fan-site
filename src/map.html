One nation, fourteen districts

Panem is not drawn in the novels, so this atlas reads the clues: a warm-water coast for District 4, grain plains for District 9, Appalachia for District 12. District borders are interpretive — the land itself is real.

Real ground

Former North America

The map is projected from actual coordinates with an Albers equal-area projection, the standard for United States maps. Rivers, ranges, lakes, and the faint outlines of the old states are all in their true places.

Interpretive borders

Drawn from the clues

The books never publish Panem’s borders. District 12 is placed in Appalachia, District 13 in the Northeast, and the Capitol in the Rockies, following the saga’s clues and the long-standing reader geography of Panem.

Not canon

A fan atlas

Borders and placements are a loving interpretation, not an official map. District contents follow the novels and films; if two districts could share a state, this atlas picks the reading that fits the story best.
