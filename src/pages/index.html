Why her books endure

Three questions at the heart of her work

Every series asks them differently — through Panem, through the Underland, and through stories for young readers.

Power & Propaganda

Who controls the story? From the Capitol’s broadcasts to the Underland’s warring factions, Collins asks how images and narratives are used to govern — and how people resist them.

War & Moral Consequence

Victory has a cost. Drawing on classical myth, gladiatorial spectacle, and war reporting, she examines what violence does to the people who choose it, survive it, and refuse it.

Youth & Agency

Young protagonists are not pawns. Whether in the arena, underground, or facing a parent’s absence, they must decide what to believe and what to do — even when adults have failed them.

Suzanne Collins

American Novelist & Screenwriter

Suzanne Collins began her writing career in 1991 as a writer for children's television before publishing the acclaimed Underland Chronicles and the world-renowned The Hunger Games series.

Drawing inspiration from classical mythology, Roman gladiatorial games, and contemporary war reportage, her novels challenge readers to examine the ethical consequences of violence, power, and media spectacle.
